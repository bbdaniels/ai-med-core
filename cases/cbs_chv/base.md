# Community-Based Surveillance: CHV Signal Detection and Reporting

## Setting and Background

- **Setting:** Kijiji Crossing -- a cluster of three small villages (A, B, and C) along a dusty road near a seasonal river
- **Community context:** Most families use shallow open wells and the river for drinking and washing. They keep small animals (goats, chickens) and trade at a weekly market beside the river.
- **Health infrastructure:** One health post in Village B; a larger health centre 15 km away on the main road
- **Role of respondent:** Community Health Volunteer (CHV) based in Village A, trained in CBS, uses a CHV notebook, meets quarterly with key informants

## Key Characters

- **Fatou** -- the CHV (respondent's role). Based in Village A.
- **Mama Asha** -- food vendor at the weekly market. Key informant in Fatou's network.
- **Imam Karim** -- religious leader in Village A. Key informant in Fatou's network.
- **Teacher Daniel** -- primary school teacher in Village B. Key informant in Fatou's network.
- **Mr. Musa** -- local-level supervisor based at the health post in Village B. Fatou reports signals to him.

## Opening Scene

It is Monday morning. The CHV goes to the weekly market to buy vegetables and stops at Mama Asha's food stall.

> (Mama Asha) Ah, welcome! You know, these last days business has been slow. Many people from the lower part of Village A and from Village C have been sick. Since Friday, they are going many times to the toilet with very watery stool. Some of them vomit. My neighbour's two children in Village C are so weak they cannot even walk to the toilet. People say it is just bad food from the market and that it will pass.

## What Mama Asha Knows (if asked further)

- She first heard about the illnesses on Saturday when customers stopped coming
- She thinks around 8 or 9 people are affected but is not sure of the exact number
- The worst cases seem to be in the lower part of Village A near the open well, and in Village C near the river
- She heard that a burial took place in Village C about a week ago with many visitors; food and water were served
- She does not know if anyone has died but says one old man in Village A "looks very bad"
- She attributes it to "bad food" or "the heat starting" -- she does not suspect a specific disease
- She has not been sick herself

## What Imam Karim Knows (if contacted)

- He has visited several families in the lower part of Village A for prayers
- He has personally seen 3 adults and 2 children who are ill with watery diarrhoea and vomiting
- The first cases he heard about began on Friday, three days ago
- One elderly man has sunken eyes, is very weak, and is struggling to stand
- All the affected households he visited use the same open well near the river
- He is worried but some elders say "this is just the normal diarrhoea that comes when the heat starts"
- He does not know the situation in Village C in detail

## What Teacher Daniel Knows (if contacted)

- Three children from his school in Village B have been absent since Monday
- He heard from their parents that the children have diarrhoea and vomiting
- Two of the absent children live in Village C; one lives in the lower part of Village A
- He does not know much about the adult cases
- He mentions that the children were fine last week at school

## Field Visit Findings -- Village A (lower area)

If the CHV visits the lower part of Village A (with or without Imam Karim):

- **3 adults and 2 children** have sudden onset watery diarrhoea (more than 5 times per day) and vomiting
- First cases began **three days ago (Friday)**
- One elderly man is **very weak, with sunken eyes**, struggling to stand. His daughter says he has not urinated since yesterday and cannot keep water down.
- All affected households use the **same open well near the river**
- The well is unprotected and close to a drainage channel
- Families have been giving home remedies (herbal tea, boiled rice water) but no ORS
- No one in these households has travelled outside the area recently

## Field Visit Findings -- Village C (near the river)

If the CHV visits Village C:

- **4 children and 1 young woman** have watery diarrhoea and vomiting
- Onset started between **Friday and Sunday**
- Families report that a **burial took place in Village C one week ago** with many visitors from surrounding areas; food and water were served at the gathering
- The young woman helped prepare food for the burial
- No one from these households has travelled outside the area in the last 3 weeks
- Two of the children are "so weak they cannot walk to the toilet" according to their mother
- Families have not sought care at the health post yet; they say it is too far and they have no transport

## Reporting to Mr. Musa

When the CHV calls or visits Mr. Musa to report:

- Mr. Musa answers the phone but the signal is weak
- He asks for key details: what is happening, where, when did it start, how many people, how severe
- He thanks the CHV for reporting quickly
- He says he will look into it and asks the CHV to keep monitoring and to encourage severely ill people to come to the health post
- He does not give a diagnosis or tell the CHV what disease it might be

## CBS Signal Reference

The following CBS signal definitions are what the CHV was trained on:

1. Two or more persons with similar severe signs/symptoms (needing medical care) from the same community, school, or workplace within one week
2. A cluster of unexplained animal deaths within one week
3. An illness with novel or rare symptoms (signs/symptoms the community has not seen before)
4. Any person with fever and rash

## Learning Objectives

After completing this exercise, the respondent should have demonstrated ability to:

- Recognize a CBS signal from community reports
- Gather relevant information from key informants and affected households
- Record a complete CHV notebook entry (date signal began, date detected, description, number affected, location, exposures)
- Report the signal to a supervisor using the Who/When/What/Where/How many/How framework
- Identify appropriate immediate actions within a CHV's scope (ORS advice, handwashing, safe water, referral of severe cases)
- Explain why immediate reporting is necessary rather than waiting for the next routine meeting
