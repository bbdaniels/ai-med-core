# Community-Based Surveillance: Supervisor Triage, Verification, and Event Reporting

## Setting and Background

- **Setting:** Kijiji Crossing -- a cluster of three small villages (A, B, and C) along a dusty road near a seasonal river
- **Community context:** Most families use shallow open wells and the river for drinking and washing. They keep small animals (goats, chickens) and trade at a weekly market beside the river.
- **Health infrastructure:** One health post in Village B; a larger health centre 15 km away on the main road
- **Role of respondent:** Local-level supervisor based at the health post in Village B, responsible for CBS triage, verification, recording events in the Signal Register, and reporting to the district surveillance office

## Key Characters

- **Mr. Musa** -- local-level supervisor (respondent's role). Based at the health post in Village B.
- **Fatou** -- Community Health Volunteer based in Village A. She has been trained in CBS and reports signals to Mr. Musa.
- **Health post nurse** -- works at the health post in Village B. Has treated patients directly.
- **Imam Karim** -- religious leader in Village A. Key informant.
- **Village C elder** -- community leader in Village C. Can be contacted by phone.
- **Mama Asha** -- food vendor at the weekly market.

## Opening Scene

It is Tuesday morning. The supervisor is at the health post reviewing notes. Yesterday evening, he received a call from Fatou, the CHV in Village A. The phone signal was weak and the call was short.

> (Fatou, recounting her phone report) I have seen many people with watery diarrhoea and vomiting in the lower part of Village A and in Village C. It started three days ago. Some people are very weak. They all use the same open well, and there was a burial in Village C last week. I wrote the details in my notebook.

## What Fatou Can Provide (if contacted for follow-up)

- She visited the lower part of Village A with Imam Karim on Monday afternoon
- In Village A lower area: 3 adults and 2 children with sudden onset watery diarrhoea (more than 5 times daily) and vomiting
- First cases began Friday
- One elderly man is very weak with sunken eyes, struggling to stand, has not urinated since the previous day
- All affected households in Village A use the same open well near the river
- In Village C: 4 children and 1 young woman with watery diarrhoea and vomiting, onset Friday to Sunday
- A burial took place in Village C one week ago with many visitors; food and water were served
- The young woman helped prepare food for the burial
- No one has travelled outside the area recently
- She recorded everything in her CHV notebook
- She has not yet given any advice to families about ORS or handwashing -- she wanted to report first

## What the Health Post Nurse Knows

- Three patients from Village A and Village C came to the health post over the weekend with severe watery diarrhoea
- One required IV fluids due to severe dehydration
- The patients described sudden onset, very watery stool many times per day, and vomiting
- The nurse treated them with ORS and IV fluids and sent them home with instructions
- She had not heard about additional cases in the community until the supervisor mentioned Fatou's report
- She has no lab testing capability at the health post

## What the Village C Elder Knows (if contacted by phone)

- He confirms there are at least 4 children with similar illness in Village C
- Many visitors attended the burial and used the same food and water sources
- He says families are worried but think it will pass
- He has not contacted the health post because "it is too far and there is no transport"
- He can help arrange a visit if the supervisor wants to come

## What Imam Karim Can Confirm (if contacted)

- He accompanied Fatou on her visits in Village A
- He personally saw the 3 adults and 2 children who are ill
- He confirms the elderly man's condition is serious -- sunken eyes, too weak to stand
- He saw that the open well is unprotected and very close to a drainage channel
- He is willing to help coordinate community communication

## Verification Visit Findings -- Village A

If the supervisor visits the lower part of Village A:

- Confirms 5 cases in 3 households (3 adults, 2 children)
- All have acute watery diarrhoea and vomiting, onset Friday
- The elderly man is severely dehydrated: sunken eyes, skin tenting, has not urinated since the day before, cannot stand without help
- The open well is unprotected, located close to a drainage channel that runs toward the river
- Families have been using home remedies but no ORS
- No one has travelled outside the area

## Verification Visit Findings -- Village C

If the supervisor visits Village C:

- Confirms 5 cases (4 children, 1 young woman)
- Onset Friday to Sunday
- The burial gathering is confirmed: many visitors, food and water served, the young woman prepared food
- Two children are very weak and cannot walk to the toilet
- Families have not visited the health post
- Water source is the river

## Triage and Verification Reference

**Triage questions:**
1. Is this signal relevant for early warning? (Could it be a genuine public health event?)
2. Is this signal new, or a duplicate of something already being handled?

**Verification questions (all three must be yes to confirm as event):**
1. Is the report accurate and true?
2. Is it reported by reliable sources?
3. Does it meet criteria for one or more CBS signals?

**CBS signal definitions:**
1. Two or more persons with similar severe signs/symptoms (needing medical care) from the same community, school, or workplace within one week
2. A cluster of unexplained animal deaths within one week
3. An illness with novel or rare symptoms (signs/symptoms the community has not seen before)
4. Any person with fever and rash

## Signal Register Fields

The Signal Register should capture:
- Date identified
- Date reported
- Name and source of report
- Location
- Signal code (from national list)
- Number of people affected
- Travel history (Y/N, where)
- Verification (True/False)
- Date verified
- Date event reported to intermediate level

## Community Backlash Context

A few days after the district team visits and begins a response (providing treatment supplies and chlorine), some community members are angry. They fear the well will be "closed forever" and accuse Fatou of "bringing trouble" by reporting. The supervisor should demonstrate supportive supervision for the CHV.

## Learning Objectives

After completing this exercise, the respondent should have demonstrated ability to:

- Triage a signal for relevance and novelty
- Conduct verification within 24 hours by contacting multiple sources (CHV, health post staff, community leaders)
- Apply the three verification questions to confirm or discard a signal as an event
- Complete a Signal Register entry with all required fields
- Report a verified event to the intermediate level (district surveillance officer) with structured information
- Demonstrate supportive supervision for a CHV facing community backlash
- Identify key messages for community engagement to maintain trust and reduce rumours
