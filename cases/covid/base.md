# Outpatient Evaluation of COVID-19 Symptoms

## Patient Background

- **Name:** Maria Santos
- **Age / Gender:** 38-year-old woman
- **Occupation / Living Situation:** Elementary school teacher; lives in a suburban apartment with her husband and two children (ages 6 and 9)
- **Marital / Family Status:** Married
- **Socioeconomic Context:** Middle income; has employer-provided health insurance
- **Setting:** Outpatient primary care clinic
- **Primary Reason for Visit:** Persistent cough, fatigue, and low-grade fever for 4 days
- **Lifestyle / Supports:** Non-smoker, occasional glass of wine on weekends; exercises regularly (jogging 3x/week, currently stopped due to illness); no drug use

## Chief Complaint & Opening Statement

> "I've had this cough and fever for a few days now. I took a home COVID test and it was positive. My school said I need to see a doctor before I can come back."

## History of Present Illness

- **Onset:** Symptoms began 4 days ago with a scratchy throat and mild body aches
- **Course:** Progressed to dry cough, low-grade fever (up to 100.8 F at home), fatigue, and mild headache; no improvement over the past 2 days
- **Associated Symptoms:** Mild loss of taste, occasional chills, nasal congestion; denies shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, or difficulty breathing
- **Aggravating Factors:** Cough worsens when lying down; fatigue worse in the afternoon
- **Relieving Factors:** Acetaminophen helps with fever and body aches; rest provides some relief
- **Functional Impact:** Unable to work for 4 days; too tired to exercise; managing basic household tasks with difficulty
- **Context:** Several students in her class were out sick last week; husband also has mild cold symptoms; children are well so far

## Past Medical History

- Mild intermittent asthma (uses albuterol inhaler PRN, rarely needed)
- Seasonal allergies
- No hospitalizations or surgeries

## Medications

- Albuterol inhaler PRN (last used 3 months ago)
- Cetirizine 10 mg daily (for allergies, seasonal)
- Acetaminophen 500 mg every 6 hours as needed (started 4 days ago for fever)
- Multivitamin daily

## Allergies

- No known drug allergies

## Family History

- Mother: type 2 diabetes, hypertension
- Father: healthy
- No family history of lung disease or blood clots

## Social History

- Lives with husband and two children
- Elementary school teacher (high exposure environment)
- Non-smoker, no vaping
- Occasional alcohol (1-2 drinks per week)
- No recreational drugs
- Up to date on COVID vaccinations (received bivalent booster 8 months ago)

## Review of Systems (Focused)

- **General:** Fatigue, mild chills, no significant weight loss
- **HEENT:** Mild nasal congestion, decreased taste, scratchy throat; no ear pain
- **Respiratory:** Dry cough, no wheezing, no shortness of breath at rest, no hemoptysis
- **Cardiac:** No chest pain, palpitations, or edema
- **GI:** Mild decrease in appetite, no nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea
- **Neuro:** Mild headache, no confusion or dizziness
- **Psych:** Anxious about missing work and potentially infecting family

## Provider Questions and Scripted SP Responses

> "Can you tell me about your symptoms?"
"It started with a sore throat and body aches about 4 days ago. Then I got this cough that won't go away, and I've been running a low fever. I'm just really tired."

> "When did you take the COVID test?"
"I took a home test the morning after my symptoms started, and it was positive. I took another one yesterday and it was still positive."

> "Are you having any trouble breathing?"
"No, not really. I mean, the cough is annoying, but I can breathe fine. I haven't needed my inhaler."

> "Have you had any chest pain or tightness?"
"No chest pain. Just general achiness."

> "How high has your fever been?"
"The highest I measured was 100.8. It usually comes down with Tylenol."

> "Have you noticed any changes in taste or smell?"
"Things taste a bit dull, but I can still smell. It's not completely gone."

> "Are you up to date on your COVID vaccines?"
"Yes, I got the updated booster about 8 months ago."

> "Do you have any chronic health conditions?"
"Just mild asthma. I hardly ever use my inhaler. And seasonal allergies."

> "Is anyone else at home sick?"
"My husband has a mild cold -- stuffy nose and a cough -- but he's not as bad as me. The kids seem fine so far."

> "Have you been able to eat and drink?"
"I've been drinking a lot of water and tea. My appetite is down, but I'm eating some."

> "Are you taking any medications right now?"
"Just Tylenol for the fever and my allergy pill. I haven't needed my inhaler."

> "What are you most worried about?"
"Honestly, I'm worried about my asthma making this worse, and about my kids getting sick. And I need to know when I can go back to work."

> "How are you managing at home?"
"It's tough. My husband is helping with the kids, but he's not feeling great either. I'm mostly resting in our bedroom."

## Physical Examination Findings

- **General:** Alert, appears mildly fatigued, no acute distress
- **Vitals:** Temp 100.2 F (37.9 C), BP 118/72, HR 88, RR 16, SpO2 97% on room air
- **HEENT:** Mild pharyngeal erythema, no exudates; nasal mucosa slightly edematous; no lymphadenopathy
- **Respiratory:** Clear to auscultation bilaterally, no wheezing or crackles; good air movement
- **Cardiac:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs
- **Abdomen:** Soft, non-tender, normal bowel sounds
- **Skin:** No rashes

## Assessment / Differential Diagnosis

1. COVID-19 infection, mild to moderate (confirmed by home rapid antigen test)
2. Mild asthma -- potential for exacerbation with viral URI
3. Viral upper respiratory infection (contributing to ongoing symptoms)

## Management Plan

- Assess Paxlovid eligibility (asthma is a risk factor; evaluate severity and timing within 5-day window)
- Symptomatic management: continue acetaminophen, adequate hydration, rest
- Monitor for respiratory worsening: return if shortness of breath, chest pain, persistent high fever, or inability to keep fluids down
- Discuss isolation guidance per current CDC recommendations
- Review return-to-work criteria with documentation for employer
- Ensure albuterol inhaler is accessible and not expired in case of asthma flare
- Follow up by phone or telehealth in 3-5 days if not improving, or sooner if worsening

## Counseling & Education Points

- Discuss current isolation and masking recommendations
- Review warning signs requiring emergency care (severe SOB, persistent chest pain, confusion, inability to stay awake)
- Encourage household mitigation strategies (masking, ventilation, separate sleeping if possible)
- Discuss expected illness course (most improve within 7-10 days)
- Address return-to-work guidance and documentation

## Emotional & Behavioral Cues

- **Tone:** Cooperative but visibly tired; speaks slowly
- **Affect:** Mildly anxious about work and family
- **Physical:** Occasional dry cough during conversation; rubs temples
- **Volunteers:** "I just don't want my kids to get this."

## Learning Objectives

- Evaluate and manage mild-moderate COVID-19 in an outpatient setting
- Assess Paxlovid eligibility based on risk factors and symptom timeline
- Provide appropriate isolation guidance and return-to-work counseling
- Identify patients at risk for complications (asthma) and plan monitoring
- Practice empathetic communication addressing patient concerns about family
