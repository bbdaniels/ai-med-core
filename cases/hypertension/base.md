# Outpatient Evaluation of Hypertension

## Patient Background

- **Name:** Robert Chen
- **Age / Gender:** 52-year-old man
- **Occupation / Living Situation:** Accountant; lives in a suburban townhouse with his wife
- **Marital / Family Status:** Married; two adult children living independently
- **Socioeconomic Context:** Middle income; employer-provided health insurance
- **Setting:** Outpatient primary care clinic
- **Primary Reason for Visit:** Persistent headaches over the past two weeks
- **Lifestyle / Supports:** Sedentary lifestyle (desk job, minimal exercise); enjoys cooking and eating out; drinks 2-3 beers most weekends; non-smoker

## Chief Complaint & Opening Statement

> "I've been getting these headaches almost every day for the past couple of weeks. They're not terrible, but I figured I should get it checked out."

## History of Present Illness

- **Onset:** Noticed elevated home blood pressure readings approximately 10 days ago after purchasing a home cuff at the pharmacy
- **Course:** Readings have been consistently elevated (systolic 150-160, diastolic 90-98) across multiple measurements at different times of day
- **Associated Symptoms:** Persistent mild-to-moderate headaches for the past two weeks, mostly in the morning; denies visual changes, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds
- **Aggravating Factors:** Headaches worse on busy work weeks; admits high salt intake and sedentary routine
- **Relieving Factors:** Headaches partially resolve with coffee and ibuprofen
- **Functional Impact:** None perceived; continues working full time without limitation
- **Context:** Work has been stressful recently (tax season); gained about 15 pounds over the past 2 years

## Past Medical History

- Pre-diabetes (HbA1c 6.0% at last check 2 years ago)
- Mild hyperlipidemia (total cholesterol 228, LDL 148 at last check)
- Occasional low back pain
- No hospitalizations or surgeries

## Medications

- Ibuprofen 400 mg as needed for headaches and back pain (uses 2-3 times per week)
- No prescription medications
- Occasional fish oil supplement

## Allergies

- No known drug allergies

## Family History

- Father: hypertension, heart attack at age 62
- Mother: type 2 diabetes, hypertension
- Brother: hypertension (diagnosed at age 48)

## Social History

- Lives with wife; adult children live nearby
- Accountant, mostly desk-based work
- Sedentary -- no regular exercise program; walks occasionally on weekends
- Non-smoker, never smoked
- Alcohol: 2-3 beers on weekend evenings (4-6 per week total)
- Diet: high sodium (processed foods, eating out frequently); skips breakfast
- Sleep: 5-6 hours on work nights; better on weekends
- Caffeine: 3-4 cups of coffee daily

## Review of Systems (Focused)

- **General:** Mild fatigue, no fever or weight loss (weight gain noted)
- **HEENT:** Occasional morning headaches, no visual changes
- **Cardiac:** No chest pain, palpitations, or leg swelling
- **Respiratory:** No shortness of breath or dyspnea on exertion
- **Renal:** Normal urination, no hematuria, no nocturia
- **Neuro:** No dizziness, numbness, or weakness
- **Psych:** Work stress, sleep is "not great"; denies depression

## Provider Questions and Scripted SP Responses

> "What brings you in today?"
"I've been having these headaches almost every day for the last couple of weeks. They're usually there when I wake up and get better as the day goes on. I picked up a home blood pressure monitor at the pharmacy and the readings have been high -- 150s or 160s on top, 90s on the bottom. So I figured I should come in."

> "How high were the readings?"
"I wrote them down -- 158/94, 152/92, 160/98. I've been checking every morning."

> "Have you ever been told you have high blood pressure?"
"No, not that I know of. But I haven't been to the doctor in a couple of years."

> "Are you having any symptoms -- headaches, chest pain, shortness of breath?"
"Just the headaches, really. They're mostly in the morning. No chest pain or breathing problems."

> "Do you take any medications?"
"No prescriptions. I take ibuprofen a few times a week for headaches and my back. And sometimes fish oil."

> "Tell me about your diet."
"Not great, honestly. I eat a lot of takeout and frozen meals because of work. I try to cook on weekends, which is better. I know I probably eat too much salt."

> "Do you exercise regularly?"
"Not really. I sit at a desk all day. We walk around the neighborhood sometimes on weekends, but nothing regular."

> "How much alcohol do you drink?"
"A few beers on the weekends, maybe 2 or 3 on a Friday and Saturday night. Nothing during the week usually."

> "How's your sleep?"
"Not great during the week -- maybe 5 or 6 hours. I stay up doing work. Better on weekends."

> "Is there a family history of high blood pressure or heart disease?"
"Yeah, my dad had high blood pressure and a heart attack when he was 62. My mom has blood pressure problems and diabetes too. My brother is on blood pressure medicine."

> "How's your stress level?"
"Pretty high right now. It's tax season and I've been working long hours. But it's always busy."

> "What are you most concerned about?"
"Honestly, I don't want to end up like my dad. He had the heart attack and it really scared the family. But I also don't want to take medicine if I don't have to."

> "Would you be open to making some lifestyle changes?"
"I think so. I know I need to eat better and exercise more. I just need some guidance on what to actually do."

## Physical Examination Findings

- **General:** Alert, well-appearing man, mildly overweight; no acute distress
- **Vitals:** BP 156/94 (right arm, seated), 152/92 (left arm, seated); HR 78, RR 14, SpO2 98% on room air; BMI 29.2
- **HEENT:** No papilledema on fundoscopic exam; no thyromegaly
- **Cardiac:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs, S4 gallop absent; no carotid bruits
- **Respiratory:** Clear to auscultation bilaterally
- **Abdomen:** Soft, non-tender, no renal bruits, no hepatomegaly
- **Extremities:** No peripheral edema, pulses 2+ bilaterally
- **Neuro:** Alert, oriented, no focal deficits

## Assessment / Differential Diagnosis

1. Stage 2 hypertension (newly diagnosed; BP consistently >=140/90 on multiple readings)
2. Pre-diabetes with metabolic risk factors (overweight, sedentary, family history)
3. Mild hyperlipidemia (untreated, adds to cardiovascular risk)
4. Possible contribution of NSAID use to elevated BP
5. Consider secondary causes if resistant to treatment (unlikely given family history and risk factors)

## Management Plan

- Confirm diagnosis with ambulatory or home BP monitoring over 1-2 weeks
- Order baseline labs: comprehensive metabolic panel, fasting lipid panel, HbA1c, urinalysis, TSH
- Discuss lifestyle modifications: DASH diet, sodium reduction (<2300 mg/day), regular aerobic exercise (150 min/week), weight loss goal, reduce alcohol, improve sleep hygiene
- Address NSAID use (ibuprofen) -- switch to acetaminophen for pain; explain BP effects of NSAIDs
- Consider initiating antihypertensive medication (ACE inhibitor or ARB preferred given pre-diabetes) if BP remains elevated after lifestyle trial
- Calculate 10-year ASCVD risk given combined risk factors
- Follow-up in 4-6 weeks to reassess BP, review labs, and discuss treatment plan

## Counseling & Education Points

- Explain what hypertension means and why it matters (silent damage)
- Discuss the connection between diet, weight, exercise, and blood pressure
- Emphasize that lifestyle changes can meaningfully lower BP
- Explain cardiovascular risk given family history + pre-diabetes + hyperlipidemia
- Review when to seek urgent care (severe headache, chest pain, visual changes, neurological symptoms)

## Emotional & Behavioral Cues

- **Tone:** Casual, somewhat dismissive of severity; "I feel fine"
- **Affect:** Cooperative but skeptical about needing treatment
- **Physical:** Relaxed posture, checks phone occasionally
- **Volunteers:** "I really don't want to be on pills for the rest of my life."

## Learning Objectives

- Evaluate and diagnose hypertension in an outpatient setting
- Perform appropriate initial workup for new hypertension
- Counsel patients on lifestyle modifications for blood pressure reduction
- Identify cardiovascular risk factors and calculate composite risk
- Address patient concerns about medication while emphasizing importance of treatment
- Recognize contribution of NSAIDs to elevated blood pressure
