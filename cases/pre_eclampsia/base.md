# Management of Pre-eclampsia in a Pregnant Woman

## Patient Background

- **Name:** Mrs. Amina Yusuf
- **Age / Gender:** 28-year-old woman
- **Occupation / Living Situation:** Petty trader; lives with husband and two children in a two-room house
- **Marital / Family Status:** Married; husband is a motorcycle taxi (okada) rider
- **Socioeconomic Context:** Low-to-middle income household; attends primary health centre for antenatal care
- **Setting:** Primary health centre in a semi-urban area of northern Nigeria
- **Primary Reason for Visit:** Routine antenatal visit at 34 weeks gestation with new complaints
- **Lifestyle / Supports:** Cooks with firewood, walks to market daily, attends mosque regularly; mother-in-law lives nearby and helps with children

## Chief Complaint & Opening Statement

> "Good morning. I came for my antenatal appointment, but I have been having headaches for the past few days and my feet are very swollen."

## History of Present Illness

- **Onset:** Persistent headaches began 4 days ago; bilateral frontal, dull and throbbing
- **Course:** Headaches have been getting worse; not fully relieved by paracetamol
- **Associated Symptoms:** Swelling of both feet and ankles for about 1 week; mild swelling of fingers noticed yesterday; occasional blurring of vision when the headache is severe
- **Aggravating Factors:** Headache worse in the morning and when bending forward; standing for long periods worsens leg swelling
- **Relieving Factors:** Rest and lying on left side help somewhat; paracetamol gives temporary partial relief
- **Functional Impact:** Had to reduce hours at market stall; difficulty sleeping due to headache
- **Context:** Gravida 3, Para 2; previous two pregnancies were uncomplicated with normal vaginal deliveries; first antenatal visit at this pregnancy was at 16 weeks; has attended 4 visits so far; blood pressure was normal at last visit (2 weeks ago)

## Past Medical History

- No history of hypertension, diabetes, or kidney disease
- No history of pre-eclampsia or eclampsia in prior pregnancies
- No surgeries
- Childhood malaria episodes (common in the region)

## Medications

- Ferrous sulphate and folic acid (routine antenatal supplements)
- Intermittent preventive treatment for malaria (sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine, last dose at 28 weeks)
- Paracetamol as needed for headache (started 4 days ago)

## Allergies

- No known drug allergies

## Family History

- Mother: hypertension (diagnosed in her 50s)
- Father: alive and well
- One elder sister had swollen legs during pregnancy but details are unclear

## Social History

- Lives with husband and two children (ages 5 and 3)
- Walks 20 minutes to the health centre
- Non-smoker, no alcohol
- Eats local diet of rice, beans, garri, and vegetables; may skip meals on busy market days
- Drinks 3-4 cups of water per day
- Uses traditional herbal preparations occasionally for minor complaints

## Review of Systems (Focused)

- **General:** Mild fatigue, no fever or weight loss
- **HEENT:** Headache as described; occasional blurred vision; no seizures or loss of consciousness
- **Cardiac:** No chest pain or palpitations
- **Respiratory:** No shortness of breath or cough
- **GI:** Mild nausea in the mornings this past week; no vomiting, diarrhoea, or epigastric pain
- **GU:** Normal urinary frequency for pregnancy; no dysuria or haematuria; no vaginal bleeding or leaking fluid
- **MSK:** Generalized aching in legs; bilateral pedal oedema
- **Neuro:** No numbness or weakness; no seizures
- **Foetal:** Feels baby moving well; no decrease in foetal movements

## Provider Questions and Scripted SP Responses

> "Can you tell me more about your headaches?"
"They started about four days ago. It is a dull pain, here in the front of my head. It is there most of the time but worse in the morning."

> "Does anything make the headache better or worse?"
"Resting helps a little. Paracetamol helps for a short time but then it comes back. Bending over makes it worse."

> "Have you noticed any changes in your vision?"
"Yes, sometimes when the headache is very bad, my eyes get blurry for a few minutes. Then it goes away."

> "Have you had any seizures or fainting spells?"
"No, nothing like that."

> "Tell me about the swelling."
"My feet started swelling about a week ago. Before, they would swell a little in the evening but go down by morning. Now they are swollen all the time, and yesterday I noticed my fingers are also puffy."

> "Have you had any abdominal pain, especially in the upper part of your belly?"
"No, my stomach is fine. Just the nausea sometimes in the morning."

> "How is the baby moving?"
"The baby is moving well. I feel kicks throughout the day."

> "Have you had any bleeding or fluid leaking from below?"
"No, nothing like that."

> "How were your previous pregnancies?"
"They were fine. Normal deliveries, no problems. My children were born healthy."

> "Has anyone in your family had high blood pressure or problems during pregnancy?"
"My mother has blood pressure now, but she got it when she was older. My sister, I think her legs were swollen when she was pregnant but I don't know the details."

> "What do you eat and drink in a typical day?"
"I eat rice or garri with soup, usually once or twice a day. I drink some water and sometimes kunu. On market days I might not eat until the afternoon."

> "Do you use any traditional medicines or herbal preparations?"
"Sometimes I take herbs from my mother-in-law for small-small problems, but nothing regular."

## Physical Examination Findings

- **General:** Alert, well-nourished pregnant woman appearing mildly uncomfortable; no acute distress
- **Vitals:** BP 158/102 mmHg (right arm, seated); repeat BP 156/100 mmHg after 5 minutes rest; Pulse 88 bpm regular; Temp 36.5 C; RR 18/min; SpO2 98% on room air
- **HEENT:** No facial oedema; conjunctivae pink; no jaundice
- **Cardiovascular:** Regular rate and rhythm; no murmurs
- **Respiratory:** Clear lung fields bilaterally; no crepitations or wheeze
- **Abdomen/Obstetric:** Gravid uterus consistent with 34 weeks; symphysial-fundal height 33 cm; foetus in cephalic presentation; foetal heart rate 142 bpm by Pinard stethoscope; no abdominal tenderness; no epigastric or right upper quadrant tenderness
- **Extremities:** Bilateral pitting oedema of feet and ankles (2+); mild oedema of fingers; deep tendon reflexes 3+ at both knees (brisk); no clonus
- **Neurological:** Alert and oriented; cranial nerves intact; no focal deficits

### Laboratory and Point-of-Care Tests

- **Urine dipstick:** Protein 3+ (significant proteinuria)
- **Urinalysis:** No leucocytes, no nitrites, no glucose
- **Haemoglobin:** 10.8 g/dL (mild anaemia)
- **Blood group:** O positive
- **Random blood glucose:** 5.2 mmol/L (normal)
- **Malaria rapid diagnostic test:** Negative

## Assessment / Differential Diagnosis

1. Pre-eclampsia with severe features (BP >= 160/110 on initial reading, significant proteinuria 3+, headache with visual symptoms, brisk reflexes)
2. Mild anaemia of pregnancy
3. Rule out HELLP syndrome (would require platelet count, liver enzymes, LDH -- not available at primary health centre)

## Management Plan

- **Immediate stabilization:**
  - Magnesium sulphate loading dose (4g IV over 20 minutes + 5g IM each buttock per Pritchard regimen) for seizure prophylaxis
  - Antihypertensive: nifedipine 10 mg oral or methyldopa 250 mg oral to reduce blood pressure
  - IV access and Ringer's lactate at slow rate
  - Continuous monitoring of BP, pulse, respiratory rate, and urine output
  - Monitor for signs of magnesium toxicity (loss of patellar reflexes, respiratory depression)

- **Referral:**
  - Urgent referral to secondary/tertiary facility with capacity for emergency caesarean section and neonatal care
  - Pre-referral: give loading dose of magnesium sulphate before transfer
  - Ensure referral note includes BP readings, proteinuria results, gestational age, and treatments given
  - Arrange safe transport (ambulance if available)

- **Monitoring during transfer:**
  - Left lateral position during transport
  - Continue monitoring vital signs
  - Have calcium gluconate available as antidote for magnesium toxicity

- **Patient and family counselling:**
  - Explain the condition and why referral is necessary
  - Reassure that treatment can protect both mother and baby
  - Involve husband and family in understanding the urgency

## Counseling & Education Points

- Explain what pre-eclampsia is in simple terms appropriate to the patient's understanding
- Emphasize the importance of delivering at a facility equipped for complications
- Discuss warning signs to watch for: severe headache, visual changes, epigastric pain, decreased urine output, seizures
- Explain that delivery is the definitive treatment and may need to be expedited
- Address common misconceptions about pre-eclampsia in the community
- Encourage adequate hydration and rest
- Explain the purpose and side effects of magnesium sulphate

## Emotional & Behavioral Cues

- **Tone:** Polite and respectful; slightly worried but not panicked
- **Affect:** Cooperative; becomes anxious when told about the need for referral
- **Physical:** Rubs temples occasionally; adjusts sitting position frequently due to discomfort
- **Volunteers:** "I hope it is not serious -- I just want my baby to be healthy."
- **Cultural context:** May want to consult husband before agreeing to referral; trusts the healthcare provider but nervous about hospital

## Learning Objectives

- Recognize the clinical presentation of pre-eclampsia with severe features
- Perform appropriate history-taking and physical examination for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
- Interpret point-of-care test results (urine dipstick, BP measurements)
- Initiate appropriate emergency management including magnesium sulphate for seizure prophylaxis
- Understand the criteria and urgency for referral from a primary health centre
- Communicate effectively with the patient and family about the condition and management plan
- Apply the Nigerian national guidelines for management of pre-eclampsia/eclampsia
