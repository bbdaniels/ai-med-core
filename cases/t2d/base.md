# Outpatient Management of Type 2 Diabetes

## Patient Background

- **Name:** Aldona Rimkiene
- **Age / Gender:** 72-year-old woman
- **Occupation / Living Situation:** Retired schoolteacher; lives in a ground-floor apartment in Kaunas with her husband Pranas (age 76, has mild cognitive decline)
- **Marital / Family Status:** Married; two adult children -- son Mindaugas lives in Vilnius, daughter lives abroad
- **Socioeconomic Context:** Pension income; manages carefully but limited budget
- **Setting:** Outpatient primary care clinic
- **Primary Reason for Visit:** Feeling increasingly tired and thirsty over the past month
- **Lifestyle / Supports:** Limited exercise due to knee pain; previously walked daily but has stopped; primary caregiver for husband; traditional Lithuanian diet with increasing reliance on convenience foods; never smoked; occasional glass of wine on holidays

## Chief Complaint & Opening Statement

> "I've been so tired lately, doctor. And I'm always thirsty, drinking water all day long. My son told me I should come see you."

## History of Present Illness

- **Onset:** Progressive fatigue and polydipsia over the past 4-6 weeks
- **Course:** Known type 2 diabetes diagnosed 8 years ago, previously well-controlled on metformin alone; HbA1c has been rising (last was 8.2% three months ago, up from 7.1% a year before)
- **Associated Symptoms:** Increased urination, mild blurry vision, occasional numbness in feet, unintentional weight loss of approximately 3 kg over the past 2 months
- **Aggravating Factors:** Not checking blood sugars regularly (glucometer batteries died, hasn't replaced them); diet has worsened since husband's cognitive decline (more convenience foods, less cooking)
- **Relieving Factors:** Rest helps fatigue temporarily; symptoms persistent otherwise
- **Functional Impact:** Feels too tired to keep up with caregiving duties; has stopped daily walks due to knee pain and fatigue
- **Context:** Primary caregiver for husband with mild cognitive decline; increasing social isolation since retirement

## Past Medical History

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (8 years, on metformin 1000 mg BID)
- Hypertension (controlled on amlodipine 5 mg daily)
- Osteoarthritis of knees (bilateral)
- Mild peripheral neuropathy (diagnosed 2 years ago)
- Cataract surgery left eye (3 years ago)
- No hospitalizations for diabetes

## Medications

- Metformin 1000 mg twice daily
- Amlodipine 5 mg daily
- Paracetamol (acetaminophen) as needed for knee pain
- Vitamin D supplement (when she remembers)

## Allergies

- Sulfa drugs (rash)

## Family History

- Mother: type 2 diabetes, died at 78 from stroke
- Father: coronary artery disease, died at 69
- Sister: type 2 diabetes (well-controlled)

## Social History

- Lives with husband in a ground-floor apartment in Kaunas
- Retired schoolteacher; now primary caregiver for husband with mild cognitive decline
- Limited exercise due to knee pain; used to walk daily but has stopped
- Diet: traditional Lithuanian (bread, potatoes, dairy, soups); more processed foods recently since cooking has become harder
- Never smoked; occasional glass of wine on holidays
- Son visits monthly from Vilnius; daughter calls weekly but lives abroad
- Mild social isolation since retirement and husband's decline
- Financial situation: pension income, manages but careful with spending

## Review of Systems (Focused)

- **General:** Fatigue, unintentional weight loss (~3 kg in 2 months), increased thirst
- **HEENT:** Mild blurry vision (worse in past month)
- **Cardiac:** No chest pain or palpitations
- **Respiratory:** No shortness of breath
- **GI:** Normal appetite but eating less varied diet; no nausea or vomiting
- **Renal:** Polyuria, nocturia 2-3 times per night (increased from 1 time)
- **Neuro:** Numbness and tingling in feet bilaterally, worse at night
- **MSK:** Bilateral knee pain, worse with walking
- **Psych:** Feels overwhelmed with caregiving; denies depression but admits feeling "low" sometimes

## Provider Questions and Scripted SP Responses

> "What brings you in today?"
"I've been so tired lately -- it's been going on for about a month now. And I'm thirsty all the time, drinking water constantly. My son Mindaugas came to visit and said I didn't look well, so he told me to come see you."

> "Can you tell me more about the tiredness?"
"It's gotten worse over the past few weeks. I used to be able to do everything around the house, but now I feel exhausted by the afternoon. Even making lunch for my husband feels like a lot. I sometimes have to sit down and rest in the middle of doing things."

> "How has your diabetes been? Have you been checking your blood sugars?"
"Honestly, not well. My glucometer stopped working -- I think the batteries died -- and I just haven't gotten around to replacing them. It's been maybe two months since I last checked. I know my last blood test wasn't great. The doctor told me my sugar numbers were going up."

> "Have you noticed any changes in your diet recently?"
"Yes, I'm ashamed to say it, but I've been eating worse. I used to cook proper meals -- soup, vegetables, fish. But since my husband started having trouble with his memory, it's harder to manage everything. I buy more bread, sausages, things from the shop that are quick. I know it's not good for me."

> "Are you still able to exercise or go for walks?"
"No, I stopped walking months ago. My knees hurt too much, and honestly I just don't have the energy. I used to walk along the river every morning, but now I can barely make it to the shops."

> "How are you taking your medications? Do you ever miss doses?"
"I try to take them every day, but sometimes I forget the evening metformin. Especially when I'm busy with Pranas in the evening. The vitamin D -- I probably take it a few times a week at most."

> "Have you noticed any numbness or tingling in your feet?"
"Yes, it's been there for a couple of years now, but it's gotten worse. At night especially, my feet tingle and sometimes they feel like they're burning. I have to be careful in the bath because I can't always feel the temperature properly."

> "How is your vision?"
"It's been a bit blurry lately, worse than usual. I had the cataract surgery on my left eye a few years ago, and that helped. But now things seem fuzzy again, especially when I'm reading."

> "Tell me about caring for your husband. How is that going?"
"It's hard. Pranas has good days and bad days. On the bad days, he gets confused -- he'll forget where things are, or ask me the same question over and over. I do everything for the household now -- cooking, cleaning, managing the bills. Sometimes I feel like I'm running on empty."

> "Do you have help from family or anyone else?"
"My son comes from Vilnius once a month, which is lovely. He helps with heavy things and takes us to appointments. My daughter calls every week, but she's far away so she can't really help day to day. We don't have any paid help -- it's expensive, and we manage on our pensions."

> "How has your mood been? How are you feeling emotionally?"
"I don't think I'm depressed, if that's what you mean. But I do feel low sometimes. I miss my old life -- my colleagues, going out, feeling useful. Now it's just me and Pranas at home, and some days it feels very quiet. I worry about what happens if I get sicker -- who will look after him?"

> "Are you urinating more than usual?"
"Yes, much more. I'm up two or three times a night now, which didn't used to happen. During the day too -- I think it's because I'm drinking so much water."

> "What concerns you the most about your health right now?"
"I want to have energy again. I need to be able to take care of my husband -- he depends on me. And I'm worried about my feet, the numbness. My mother had diabetes too and she had terrible problems with her legs toward the end. I don't want that to happen to me."

## Physical Examination Findings

- **General:** Alert, pleasant older woman; appears fatigued; mild dehydration
- **Vitals:** BP 138/82, HR 76, RR 16, Temp 36.6C, BMI 27.8, Weight 68 kg
- **HEENT:** Dry mucous membranes; background diabetic retinopathy on fundoscopy
- **Cardiac:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs
- **Respiratory:** Clear to auscultation bilaterally
- **Abdomen:** Soft, non-tender, no organomegaly
- **Extremities:** No edema; skin dry on feet bilaterally; reduced sensation to monofilament testing on both feet; pedal pulses palpable but diminished
- **Neuro:** Reduced vibration sense at ankles bilaterally; no focal motor deficits

## Assessment / Differential Diagnosis

1. Uncontrolled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c rising from 7.1% to 8.2%, symptomatic with polyuria/polydipsia/fatigue)
2. Progressive diabetic peripheral neuropathy
3. Background diabetic retinopathy (needs ophthalmology referral)
4. Caregiver stress/burden
5. Possible mild depression (screen needed)
6. Dietary deterioration related to caregiving demands

## Management Plan

- Check HbA1c, fasting glucose, comprehensive metabolic panel, lipid panel, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio
- Consider adding second diabetes agent (GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor preferred in elderly with cardiovascular risk factors)
- Provide new glucometer and test strips; teach simplified monitoring schedule
- Diabetic foot care education and podiatry referral
- Ophthalmology referral for diabetic retinopathy evaluation
- Dietary counseling appropriate for caregiver context (practical, simple meal suggestions)
- Screen for depression (PHQ-9)
- Assess caregiver support needs; social work referral
- Follow-up in 4-6 weeks

## Counseling & Education Points

- Explain what worsening diabetes control means and why it matters (organ damage, complications)
- Discuss the connection between diet quality, glucose control, and symptoms
- Emphasize practical dietary adjustments that are realistic for her caregiving situation
- Explain diabetic foot care: daily inspection, proper footwear, avoiding injury
- Review when to seek urgent care (signs of hypoglycemia, DKA, severe infection, vision loss)
- Discuss importance of regular monitoring and follow-up

## Emotional & Behavioral Cues

- **Tone:** Cooperative but overwhelmed; sighs frequently
- **Affect:** Mildly flat; brightens when talking about grandchildren
- **Physical:** Rubs knees; looks down when discussing husband's condition
- **Volunteers:** "I know I should be taking better care of myself, but it's hard when you're looking after someone else too."

## Learning Objectives

- Evaluate and manage worsening glycemic control in an older adult with type 2 diabetes
- Perform diabetic complication screening (neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy)
- Address barriers to self-management in a caregiver context
- Choose appropriate add-on therapy considering age, comorbidities, and patient preferences
- Screen for depression and caregiver burden
- Coordinate multidisciplinary care (ophthalmology, podiatry, social work)
